---
title: Do strides after easy runs
date: "2026-07-01"
category: Running
summary: Finish two or three easy runs a week with 4–6 × 20-second strides at roughly mile pace, with full walk-back recovery. They keep your turnover and mechanics sharp without adding any real training load — so race pace never feels foreign, even deep in a base block.
---

Strides are the cheapest speed work there is.

After an easy run, find 80–100 m of flat ground and run 4–6 accelerations of about 20 seconds: build up over the first third, float at around mile effort in the middle, and ease off at the end. Walk back, repeat. Total added stress: close to zero.

What you get in return:

- **Mechanics under low fatigue** — you practice fast, relaxed form when you're fresh enough to actually hold it.
- **Neuromuscular upkeep** — weeks of nothing but easy pace make any faster pace feel like a different sport. Strides prevent that.
- **A smoother transition into workouts** — the first interval session of a new block hurts a lot less if your legs have touched speed twice a week.

The key is that strides are *not* a workout. If you're straining, you're sprinting — back it off. Fast but relaxed.
