---
title: Seed everything, log the seed
date: "2026-10-01"
category: Research
summary: An experiment you can't reproduce is an anecdote. Set the seed for every RNG in play (Python, NumPy, PyTorch, CUDA), log it with the config, and run important results with three different seeds — if the effect doesn't survive a seed change, it was never real.
---

Every ML framework has several random number generators running at once, and forgetting one is enough to make a result unrepeatable:

```python
def seed_everything(seed: int):
    random.seed(seed)
    np.random.seed(seed)
    torch.manual_seed(seed)          # also seeds CUDA on current device
    torch.cuda.manual_seed_all(seed) # all devices
```

For strict determinism on GPU you also need `torch.use_deterministic_algorithms(True)` and to accept the speed cost — usually only worth it when debugging a divergence.

Two habits matter more than the boilerplate:

- **Log the seed with the run.** A seed you didn't record is a seed you don't have. It belongs in the same config dump as the learning rate.
- **Never report a single-seed result.** Run anything that goes in a table with at least three seeds and look at the spread. A surprising number of "improvements" in the literature are within seed variance of the baseline — don't add to them.

The point isn't purity, it's cheap insurance: when a reviewer, a colleague, or you-in-six-months asks "can you rerun that?", the answer is one command.
