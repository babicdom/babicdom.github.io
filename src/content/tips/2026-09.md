---
title: Read papers in three passes, not one
date: "2026-09-01"
category: Research
summary: "First pass: title, abstract, figures, conclusion — five minutes, decide if it's worth more. Second pass: full read, skipping proofs, writing one paragraph in your own words. Third pass (rare): re-derive or re-implement the core idea. Most papers should never survive past the first pass."
---

The mistake is treating every paper like it deserves a linear front-to-back read. Almost none do.

**Pass one — five minutes.** Title, abstract, headings, every figure and its caption, then the conclusion. Papers are written to survive exactly this pass: the figures usually *are* the contribution. Decide: discard, keep for reference, or read properly. Expect to discard most.

**Pass two — an hour.** Read the whole thing, skip the proofs and the related-work padding. The non-negotiable part: write a single paragraph, in your own words, answering *what did they do, what's the one idea that made it work, and what would break it*. If you can't write the paragraph, you didn't understand the paper — go back or admit it goes in the reference pile.

**Pass three — days.** Only for the handful of papers your own work builds on: re-derive the math or re-implement the method on a toy problem. This is where you find the assumptions the authors didn't state — usually in what the ablations *didn't* test.

Keep the pass-two paragraphs in one searchable place with the BibTeX entry. Six months later, that file is worth more than your memory of any of the papers.
