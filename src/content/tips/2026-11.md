---
title: Keep 80% of your running actually easy
date: "2026-11-01"
category: Running
summary: The most common self-coaching error is running easy days too hard and hard days too easy, until everything is the same grey medium effort. Keep roughly 80% of weekly volume conversational — if you can't speak in full sentences, it's not an easy run — and make the remaining 20% genuinely count.
---

Look at the training of almost any elite endurance group and the split is roughly the same: about 80% of volume at low intensity, 20% at moderate-to-high. Not because easy running is magic, but because it's the only intensity you can absorb in large amounts while still recovering enough to hit the hard sessions properly.

The failure mode for self-coached runners is intensity creep: easy runs drift 30 seconds per km too fast, so Tuesday's workout gets compromised, so the workout is run at 90% of what it should be, so everything converges to the same medium effort — hard enough to accumulate fatigue, too easy to force adaptation.

Practical guardrails:

- **The talk test beats any watch metric.** Full sentences, comfortably — not gasped fragments.
- **Slower than feels necessary is usually right.** If in doubt on an easy day, back off.
- **Protect the hard days.** The point of going easy is to show up to intervals able to actually run them. If your workout paces are sagging, the fix is almost always easier easy days, not harder workouts.

Count your weekly minutes by intensity once. Most people who think they run 80/20 are living at 60/40.
